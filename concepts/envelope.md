# Envelope

> One word was doing two jobs. **FD-EV** reserves **"envelope"** for the thing you *authorize*, and renames the thing you *monitor* to the **operating band**.

Across the corpus "envelope" named two different kinds of thing, and a reader couldn't always tell which: a region within which an above-line action is authorized in advance, and a region of normal operation whose violation is a runtime event to detect and report.

- **Pre-authorized envelope** (reserved) — an object of *authorization*. A qualified human defines it, its completeness is proven under [R2](advance-discharge.md), and a change *to* it is an [R3](advance-discharge.md) event. This is the sense the determinations use.
- **Operating band** (renamed) — an object of *monitoring*. Its boundary is an operating limit; encroachment toward it is detected and reported. This is the entrenched process-engineering sense.

The test is the kind of obligation in view: *who authorized it, and who may change it* → **pre-authorized envelope**; *where the limits are, and what to detect when operation approaches them* → **operating band**. One physical region can carry both names, each used where its obligation is in view.

Governed by **FD-EV**, a Foundational Definition companion to [FD-BL](bright-line.md) and FD-LD, resolving FD-BL's §6.3.
